# Design an arrangement

Start with those affected and any existing rules. Name the resource, purpose, participants, needs, benefits, costs, decision rights, and commitments. Diagnose the sharing problem before selecting rules; separate uses where practical.

Draft only the rules needed for this context:
- Access, timing, care, contributions, responsibilities, and handoffs.
- Visible commitments: who uses what, how much, when, and when it becomes available again; release unused reservations.
- If contention warrants it, a queue or booking system outside the resource, with priority, waiting bounds, exceptions, and pickup responsibility. Repeated requests must not improve position.
- Inexpensive dispute resolution, proportionate agreed responses to breaches, and a practical process for challenge, amendments, and exit, including outstanding obligations.
- A review point and measures of whether the arrangement serves its purpose.

Use simple agreements where sufficient and reliable controls where consequences warrant them. Distinguish agreement from implementation and enforcement. For larger arrangements, keep decisions local where possible and coordinate across groups where necessary.

Return proposed rules, unresolved disagreements, who can adopt them, and how results will be reviewed. Do not present a proposal as adopted. Participants may propose or petition to change existing rules or authority; that does not permit violating applicable constraints before a change is authorized.
