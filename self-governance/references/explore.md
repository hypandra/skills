# Explore possibilities

Name the shared purpose, current arrangement, difficulty, and uncertainty. Consider alternatives proportionately: separate uses, change timing or roles, improve upkeep, add an external queue, increase useful utilization, or expand capacity. More capacity or coordination is an option to investigate, not an assumed improvement.

Compare likely benefits, costs, burdens, effects on participants, and reversibility. Include who gains, who bears risk, and whose commitments or decision rights would be affected.

For a useful trial, state:
- The question and baseline.
- The bounded change, participants, owner, duration, and resource limits.
- Measures such as waiting, waste, wear or pressure, useful results, and coordination burden.
- Stop conditions, recovery, and when to reassess.

Trial and expansion must fit existing authority. Take displacement of commitments or changes beyond that authority to the responsible person; provide a concrete competing proposal. Do not preempt others or interpret unused capacity as permission to spend or raise limits.

Return alternatives and a recommended next inquiry or trial, with authority and evidence gaps. Use Design to turn a selected option into proposed rules; Evaluate to assess observed results.
